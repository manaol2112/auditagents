# SOX ICFR / ITGC Risk and Control Matrix (RCM): Fields Required or Implied by Audit Standards, Regulators, and Practice (vendor-independent)

Labeling convention used throughout:
- **[STD]** = required or explicitly called for by a standard or regulator text (PCAOB AS, SEC release). PCAOB standards use "should" = presumptively mandatory responsibility for the auditor (not management).
- **[STAFF]** = PCAOB/SEC staff guidance or inspection observations (not a standard, but sets inspection expectations).
- **[PRACTICE]** = common practice from firms, IIA, ISACA, CAQ, practitioner sources.
- **[INFERENCE]** = my reasoning from the cited material.

Important framing point: **no standard prescribes an "RCM" or a column list.** The RCM is a practice artifact. The standards specify *what the auditor must understand, identify, and evaluate*, and the SEC specifies that management must keep "reasonable support" and documentation of control design. Each RCM field exists because it carries one of those required data points. Source-retrieval notes: pcaobus.org blocks automated fetches (Cloudflare); AS 2201/2110/1105/2315 text was read from Wayback Machine 2025 captures of the official pcaobus.org pages and from the original PCAOB Release 2007-005A (Auditing Standard No. 5, the same paragraph numbers as AS 2201). SEC Release 33-8810 and PCAOB Staff Audit Practice Alert No. 11 were read in full from the official PDFs.

## Q1. What do PCAOB AS 2201, AS 2110, AS 2315, AS 1105, SEC Release 33-8810 and COSO 2013 imply an RCM must capture?

### Takeaway
Read together, the standards require a traceable chain: **significant account/disclosure → relevant assertion → risk of material misstatement ("what could go wrong", including fraud) → control(s) that address it → control characteristics that drive risk and testing (nature manual/automated/IT-dependent, frequency, performer and their competence/authority, precision, complexity/judgment, reliance on ITGCs, information/IPE used, evidence of operation)**. ITGCs are in scope only as they support those controls (program development, program changes, computer operations, access to programs and data). Every core RCM column maps to one of these required data points.

### Cited Findings

**AS 2201 (Audit of ICFR integrated with FS audit)**
- **Significant accounts and relevant assertions.** AS 2201.28: "The auditor should identify significant accounts and disclosures and their relevant assertions. Relevant assertions are those financial statement assertions that have a reasonable possibility of containing a misstatement that would cause the financial statements to be materially misstated." The assertions listed are Existence or occurrence; Completeness; Valuation or allocation; Rights and obligations; Presentation and disclosure. A Note permits assertions that differ from this list if controls over the pertinent risks are selected and tested. [STD] — [PCAOB AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201); [PCAOB Release 2007-005A (AS No. 5), ¶28](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Risk factors for significant accounts.** AS 2201.29 lists: size and composition of the account; susceptibility to misstatement due to errors or fraud; volume of activity, complexity, and homogeneity of transactions; nature of the account; accounting and reporting complexities; exposure to losses; possibility of significant contingent liabilities; related party transactions; changes from the prior period. [STD] — [PCAOB Release 2007-005A ¶29](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Risk statement = "what could go wrong".** AS 2201.30: the auditor "should determine the likely sources of potential misstatements that would cause the financial statements to be materially misstated," and "might determine the likely sources of potential misstatements by asking himself or herself 'what could go wrong?' within a given significant account or disclosure." [STD] — [same, ¶30](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Components can carry different risks.** AS 2201.32: components of an account "might be subject to significantly differing risks. If so, different controls might be necessary." (Supports risk records at sub-account/sub-process granularity.) [STD] — [same, ¶32](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Multi-location.** AS 2201.33: significant accounts are identified on the consolidated statements, then Appendix B is applied for multiple-location scoping. (Supports a location/business-unit field.) [STD] — [same, ¶33](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Process flow, points of misstatement, fraud, asset safeguarding.** AS 2201.34 objectives: understand the flow of transactions for relevant assertions "including how these transactions are initiated, authorized, processed, and recorded"; identify the points "at which a misstatement – including a misstatement due to fraud – could arise"; "Identify the controls that management has implemented to address these potential misstatements"; and identify controls over "prevention or timely detection of unauthorized acquisition, use, or disposition of the company's assets." [STD] — [same, ¶34](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **IT is integrated, not separate.** AS 2201.36 (current codification): "The auditor also should understand how IT affects the company's flow of transactions. The auditor should apply paragraph .29 and Appendix B of AS 2110..." Note: "The identification of risks and controls within IT is not a separate evaluation. Instead, it is an integral part of the top-down approach..." (Original AS 5 text referenced AU 319; the current text references AS 2110.) [STD] — [PCAOB AS 2201 (Wayback capture of pcaobus.org)](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201)
- **Walkthroughs** follow a transaction "from origination through the company's processes, including information systems, until it is reflected in the company's financial records, using the same documents and information technology that company personnel use" (AS 2201.37). [STD] — [PCAOB Release 2007-005A ¶37](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Control selection is risk/assertion-driven, many-to-many, label-agnostic.** AS 2201.39: test controls "important to the auditor's conclusion about whether the company's controls sufficiently address the assessed risk of misstatement to each relevant assertion." AS 2201.40: "There might be more than one control that addresses the assessed risk of misstatement to a particular relevant assertion; conversely, one control might address the assessed risk of misstatement to more than one relevant assertion"; redundant controls need not be tested. AS 2201.41: selection depends on whether controls address the risk "rather than on how the control is labeled (e.g., entity-level control, transaction-level control, control activity, monitoring control, preventive control, detective control)." [STD] — [same, ¶39–41](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Design effectiveness requires knowing the control objective and the performer's authority/competence.** AS 2201.42: design is effective if controls "operated as prescribed by persons possessing the necessary authority and competence ... satisfy the company's control objectives and can effectively prevent or detect errors or fraud." AS 2201.44: operating effectiveness includes "whether the person performing the control possesses the necessary authority and competence." [STD] — [same, ¶42, ¶44](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Control objective definition.** AS 2201.A2: "A control objective ... generally relates to a relevant assertion and states a criterion for evaluating whether the company's control procedures in a specific area provide reasonable assurance that a misstatement or omission in that relevant assertion is prevented or detected by controls on a timely basis." [STD] — [same, ¶A2](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Design deficiency = missing control.** AS 2201.A3: a design deficiency exists when "(a) a control necessary to meet the control objective is missing or (b) an existing control is not properly designed." (An RCM risk with no linked control is, on its face, a potential design gap.) [STD] — [same, ¶A3](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Risk-associated-with-the-control factors (the strongest basis for control attribute columns).** AS 2201.47: "The nature and materiality of misstatements that the control is intended to prevent or detect; The inherent risk associated with the related account(s) and assertion(s); Whether there have been changes in the volume or nature of transactions...; Whether the account has a history of errors; The effectiveness of entity-level controls...; **The nature of the control and the frequency with which it operates**; **The degree to which the control relies on the effectiveness of other controls (e.g., the control environment or information technology general controls)**; **The competence of the personnel who perform the control or monitor its performance and whether there have been changes in key personnel**; **Whether the control relies on performance by an individual or is automated** (i.e., an automated control would generally be expected to be lower risk if relevant information technology general controls are effective); and **The complexity of the control and the significance of the judgments that must be made in connection with its operation**." [STD] — [same, ¶47](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Evidence of operation determines testability.** AS 2201.50 orders tests from least to most evidence: "inquiry, observation, inspection of relevant documentation, and re-performance"; "Inquiry alone does not provide sufficient evidence." AS 2201.51: the nature of tests "depends, to a large degree, on the nature of the control to be tested, including whether the operation of the control results in documentary evidence of its operation." [STD] — [same, ¶50–51](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Entity-level controls vary in precision** (AS 2201.23): some have "indirect" effect; some monitor other controls "but not at a level of precision that would, by themselves, sufficiently address the assessed risk"; some "operate at a level of precision that would adequately prevent or detect on a timely basis misstatements to one or more relevant assertions." AS 2201.24 lists ELC types: control environment; management override; risk assessment process; centralized processing/shared services; monitoring results of operations; monitoring other controls (incl. internal audit, audit committee, self-assessment); period-end financial reporting process; policies on significant business control and risk management. [STD] — [same, ¶23–24](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **ITGC domains named in the standard (benchmarking).** AS 2201.B29: "If general controls over program changes, access to programs, and computer operations are effective and continue to be tested, and if the auditor verifies that the automated application control has not changed since the auditor established a baseline..." the auditor may benchmark. B30: automated controls "may be dependent upon the related files, tables, data, and parameters" (e.g., a rate table). B31 factors: extent the control "can be matched to a defined program within an application"; application stability; availability and reliability of a report of program compilation dates. B33: reestablish baseline considering "controls over application and system software acquisition and maintenance, access controls and computer operations." (Supports fields: application/system, program/configuration reference, baseline date, dependency on master data/tables.) [STD] — [same, ¶B28–B33](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)
- **Pending amendments:** The PCAOB page notes amendments to AS 2201 ¶.09 and a new ¶.99 effective December 15, 2026 (PCAOB Release No. 2024-005); these do not change the RCM-relevant paragraphs above. [STD] — [PCAOB AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201)

**AS 2110 (Identifying and Assessing Risks of Material Misstatement)**
- **Risk record content.** AS 2110.59: identify risks at FS and assertion level; "Evaluate the types of potential misstatements that could result from the identified risks and the accounts, disclosures, and assertions that could be affected"; "Assess the likelihood of misstatement, including the possibility of multiple misstatements, and the magnitude of potential misstatement"; identify significant accounts and relevant assertions; "Determine whether any of the identified and assessed risks of material misstatement are significant risks." Note: significance of an account/assertion "is based on inherent risk, without regard to the effect of controls." (Directly supports risk fields: affected accounts, assertions, likelihood, magnitude, significant-risk flag — assessed on an inherent basis.) [STD] — [PCAOB AS 2110](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2110)
- **Fraud.** AS 2110.65–.69 require evaluating fraud risk factors, including omitted/incomplete disclosures (.67), a presumed fraud risk in revenue recognition (.68), and the risk of management override (.69). AS 2110.71: "A fraud risk is a significant risk." (Supports a fraud-risk flag and a significant-risk flag on risks.) [STD] — [PCAOB AS 2110](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2110)
- **Information system understanding.** AS 2110.28: understand "classes of transactions ... significant to the financial statements"; "procedures, within both automated and manual systems, by which those transactions are initiated, authorized, processed, recorded, and reported"; related accounting records and "specific accounts in the financial statements"; and for estimates, "the methods used, which may include models; the data and assumptions used, including the source...; and the extent to which the company uses third parties." (Supports process/sub-process, significant class of transactions, system, service-organization fields.) [STD] — [PCAOB AS 2110](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2110)
- **Manual vs automated and ITGCs.** AS 2110.B1: the auditor "should obtain an understanding of the extent of manual controls and automated controls used by the company, including the IT general controls that are important to the effective operations of the automated controls." B3: IT systems "may include controls related to the relevant assertions ... or may be critical to the effective functioning of manual controls that depend on IT." B4 lists IT risks: inaccurate processing; unauthorized access to data; IT personnel access beyond duties (SoD breakdown); unauthorized changes to master files; unauthorized changes to systems or programs; failure to make necessary changes; inappropriate manual intervention; loss of data. B5: understand how the company responded to IT risks. B6: test "consistency in the application of those manual controls." (B4 is effectively a standard-sourced ITGC risk library.) [STD] — [PCAOB AS 2110](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2110)
- **Pending amendments:** AS 2110 ¶.05 and ¶.41 amendments are effective December 15, 2026 per the PCAOB page; not RCM-relevant on their face. [STD] — [PCAOB AS 2110](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2110)

**AS 1105 (Audit Evidence) — IPE / information produced by the company, and assertions**
- AS 1105.10 (pre-2025 text): "When using information produced by the company as audit evidence, the auditor should evaluate whether the information is sufficient and appropriate for purposes of the audit by performing procedures to: Test the accuracy and completeness of the information, or test the controls over the accuracy and completeness of that information; and Evaluate whether the information is sufficiently precise and detailed for purposes of the audit." [STD] — [PCAOB AS 1105](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS1105)
- **Amendment status:** the PCAOB page states that amendments to AS 1105 ¶¶.07, .08, .10, .13, .14, .15, .19, .A8 and new ¶.10A (PCAOB Release No. 2024-007, technology-assisted analysis) "will be effective for audits of financial statements for fiscal years beginning on or after December 15, 2025" — i.e., they apply to calendar-2026 audits. I was not able to retrieve the amended ¶.10 wording verbatim (see Gaps). [STD] — [PCAOB AS 1105](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS1105)
- AS 1105.11 assertion definitions: Existence or occurrence ("Assets or liabilities of the company exist at a given date, and recorded transactions have occurred during a given period"); Completeness ("All transactions and accounts that should be presented in the financial statements are so included"); Valuation or allocation ("...included in the financial statements at appropriate amounts"); Rights and obligations ("The company holds or controls rights to the assets, and liabilities are obligations of the company at a given date"); Presentation and disclosure ("properly classified, described, and disclosed"). AS 1105.12 allows alternative assertion sets if sufficient. [STD] — [PCAOB AS 1105](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS1105)

**AS 2315 (Audit Sampling) — why frequency and evidence fields matter**
- AS 2315.31: in planning a test-of-controls sample the auditor considers the objective of the test, tolerable deviation rate, allowable risk of assessing control risk too low, and "Characteristics of the population." AS 2315.32: "Sampling generally is not applicable to tests of controls that depend primarily on appropriate segregation of duties or that otherwise provide no documentary evidence of performance." AS 2315.38: sample size depends on tolerable rate, likely rate of deviations, and allowable risk. AS 2315.39: "all items in the population should have an opportunity to be selected." (Frequency defines population size; evidence type determines whether sampling applies. The standard does **not** give frequency-based sample-size tables.) [STD] — [PCAOB AS 2315](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2315)

**SEC Release 33-8810 (2007 interpretive guidance for management)**
- **Financial reporting elements and "what could go wrong".** Management considers "the sources and potential likelihood of misstatements in financial reporting elements" and "may find it useful to consider 'what could go wrong' within a financial reporting element." Footnote 28: "Companies may use 'control objectives,' which provide specific criteria against which to evaluate the effectiveness of controls." [STD-SEC interpretive] — [SEC Release 33-8810, §II.A.1](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)
- **Preventive vs detective** defined at fn. 30: "Preventive controls have the objective of preventing the occurrence of errors or fraud that could result in a misstatement of the financial statements. Detective controls have the objective of detecting errors or fraud that has already occurred..." [STD-SEC] — [SEC Release 33-8810](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)
- **Many-to-many and no redundancy:** "there may be more than one control that addresses the financial reporting risks for a financial reporting element; conversely, one control might address the risks of more than one financial reporting element. It is not necessary to identify all controls that may exist or identify redundant controls." [STD-SEC] — [SEC Release 33-8810](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)
- **Nature taxonomy (automated / IT-dependent / hybrid).** "Controls ... may be automated [fn34: e.g., application controls that perform automated matching, error checking or edit checking], dependent upon IT functionality [fn35: e.g., consistent application of a formula or performance of a calculation and posting correct balances], or a combination of both manual and automated procedures [fn36: e.g., a control that manually investigates items contained in a computer generated exception report]." [STD-SEC] — [SEC Release 33-8810, §II.A.1.d](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)
- **ITGC domains.** "management only needs to evaluate those IT general controls that are necessary for the proper and consistent operation of other controls designed to adequately address financial reporting risks. For example, management might consider whether certain aspects of IT general control areas, such as **program development, program changes, computer operations, and access to programs and data**, apply..." Fn 37: these areas are examples, not necessarily all applicable. "it is unnecessary to evaluate IT general controls that primarily pertain to efficiency or effectiveness of a company's operations." [STD-SEC] — [SEC Release 33-8810, §II.A.1.d](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)
- **ELC precision.** "Entity-level controls may be designed to operate at the process, application, transaction or account-level and at a level of precision that would adequately prevent or detect on a timely basis misstatements"; a monitoring ELC may be inadequate "if the amount of potential misstatement that could exist before being detected by the monitoring control is too high." Fn 33 distinguishes "directly" vs "indirectly" related controls. [STD-SEC] — [SEC Release 33-8810, §II.A.1.c](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)
- **Documentation requirement (management).** "management must maintain reasonable support for its assessment. Documentation of the design of the controls management has placed in operation to adequately address the financial reporting risks, including the entity-level and other pervasive elements necessary for effective ICFR, is an integral part of the reasonable support." Form is flexible ("policy manuals, process models, flowcharts, job descriptions, documents, internal memorandums, forms, etc"); documentation "should be focused on those controls that management concludes are adequate to address the financial reporting risks." It also evidences that controls "are capable of being communicated to those responsible for their performance, and are capable of being monitored." [STD-SEC] — [SEC Release 33-8810, §II.A.1.e](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)
- **ICFR risk = misstatement risk × risk of control failure** (diagram "Determining the Sufficiency of Evidence Based on ICFR Risk," Low/Medium/High). Misstatement risk factors: materiality and whether the element "(1) involves judgment...; (2) is susceptible to fraud; (3) has complex accounting requirements; (4) experiences change in the nature or volume...; or (5) is sensitive to changes in environmental factors." Control-failure factors: "The type of control (that is, manual or automated) and the frequency with which it operates; The complexity of the control; The risk of management override; The judgment required to operate the control; The competence of the personnel who perform the control or monitor its performance; Whether there have been changes in key personnel...; The nature and materiality of misstatements that the control is intended to prevent or detect; The degree to which the control relies on the effectiveness of other controls (for example, IT general controls); and The evidence of the operation of the control from prior year(s)." Related party transactions, critical accounting policies and estimates "generally would be assessed as having a higher misstatement risk." [STD-SEC] — [SEC Release 33-8810, §II.B.1](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)

**PCAOB Staff Audit Practice Alert No. 11 (Oct. 24, 2013) [STAFF]**
- **Precision factors for management review / entity-level controls:** "Objective of the review"; "Level of aggregation" (e.g., revenue by location/product line more precise than total); "Consistency of performance"; "Correlation to relevant assertions"; "Predictability of expectations"; "Criteria for investigation" ("the threshold for investigating deviations or differences from expectations relative to materiality is an indication of a control's precision"). — [PCAOB SAPA 11](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)
- **Design evaluation of a management review control requires:** (a) whether it satisfies the control objective and addresses the RMM to the relevant assertion; (b) precision factors incl. "objective of the review and the appropriateness of the expectations, level of aggregation, and criteria for investigation"; (c) "The steps involved in identifying, investigating, and resolving significant differences from expectations"; (d) "The person(s) who performs the control, including the competence and authority"; (e) "The frequency of performance of the control, that is, whether the review occurs often enough to prevent or detect misstatements before they have a material effect"; (f) "The information used in the review, for example, whether the review uses system-generated data or reports." Also: "to assess whether a control is effectively designed, it is important to identify the risk of material misstatement to the relevant assertion ... that the control is intended to address." (This is the closest thing to an official "required attribute list" for a control record.) — [PCAOB SAPA 11](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)
- **IT-dependent controls and IPE:** "if the auditor selects an IT-dependent control for testing, the auditor should test the IT-dependent controls and the IT controls on which the selected control relies"; "if a control selected for testing uses system-generated data or reports, the effectiveness of the control depends in part on the controls over the accuracy and completeness of the system-generated data or reports." Inspection failures cited: not testing ITGCs over the applications generating reports; not testing "the logic of the queries (or parameters) used to extract data"; IT-dependent controls using "customized data or queries that were not subject to the ITGCs the firms tested." IT deficiencies must be evaluated for their effect on dependent controls, possibly "across multiple accounts." — [PCAOB SAPA 11](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)
- **Interim testing / roll-forward:** roll-forward evidence depends on "the specific control tested at an interim date, including the risks associated with the control and the nature of the control." — [PCAOB SAPA 11](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)

**PCAOB Spotlight on Data and Reports (April 2024) [STAFF]**
- "Approximately 17% of the total comment forms for each of the 2021 and 2022 inspection cycles contained deficiencies where the auditor did not perform sufficient procedures to test (or sufficiently test controls over) the accuracy and completeness of IPC [information produced by the company]." Observed deficiency: auditor "did not identify and test controls over the accuracy and completeness of data used by the control owner in the operation of a control." IPC includes "invoices issued by the company, shipping documents created by the company, and other data and reports from company information technology systems." — [PCAOB Spotlight: Inspection Observations Related to Auditor Use of Data and Reports](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/documents/data-and-reports-spotlight.pdf)

- Principle 11: "The organization selects and develops general control activities over technology to support the achievement of objectives," with points of focus including determining "dependency between the use of technology in business process and technology general controls" and establishing technology infrastructure, security management, and technology acquisition/development/maintenance control activities. [Framework, via secondary] — [Peck, COSO Components (Univ. of Illinois SIAAB 2019)](https://siaab.audits.uillinois.edu/UserFiles/Servers/Server_4315/File/SIAAB%20Documentation/Fall%20Conference/Presentations/2019/Peck-COSO-Components.pdf); [Deloitte COSO placemat (Scribd copy)](https://www.scribd.com/document/469434303/COSO-Placemat-Deloitte-pdf)

- Principle 12 points of focus include "Establishes Responsibility and Accountability for Executing Policies and Procedures" (12.CA.49) and "Performs in a Timely Manner" (12.CA.50) — "Responsible personnel perform control activities in a timely manner as defined by the policies and procedures." (Basis for owner and timing/deadline fields.) [Framework, via secondary] — [Peck, COSO Components](https://siaab.audits.uillinois.edu/UserFiles/Servers/Server_4315/File/SIAAB%20Documentation/Fall%20Conference/Presentations/2019/Peck-COSO-Components.pdf); [AAA COSO resource](https://aaahq.org/node/35193)
- Points of focus are guidance, not a checklist requiring separate assessment of each. — [NYSICA COSO training](https://www.nysica.com/uploads/3/4/8/5/34855847/coso_training_-_new_york_state.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Standards-derived "must capture" data points [INFERENCE from STD/STAFF]:**
  1. Significant account/disclosure (and significant class of transactions / process) — AS 2201.28–.33, AS 2110.28.
  2. Relevant assertion(s) per account — AS 2201.28, AS 1105.11.
  3. Risk of material misstatement described as a "what could go wrong" at assertion level, with likelihood and magnitude, an inherent-basis significant-risk flag and a fraud-risk flag — AS 2201.30, AS 2110.59/.65–.71.
  4. Control(s) mapped many-to-many to risks/assertions — AS 2201.39–.40; SEC 33-8810.
  5. Control objective (criterion tied to an assertion) — AS 2201.A2 (optional for management per SEC fn 28, but the auditor uses it for design evaluation).
  6. Control nature (manual / automated / IT-dependent manual) — AS 2201.47, AS 2110.B1, SEC 33-8810 fn 34–36.
  7. Frequency — AS 2201.47, SEC 33-8810, SAPA 11(e); drives population for AS 2315 sampling.
  8. Performer (and reviewer/monitor), with authority/competence — AS 2201.42/.44/.47, SAPA 11(d), COSO Principle 12.
  9. Complexity/judgment, management-override exposure — AS 2201.47, SEC 33-8810.
  10. Precision attributes (threshold for investigation, level of aggregation, expectation basis) — AS 2201.23, SAPA 11. Essential for review/ELC controls.
  11. Information used (IPE/system reports, queries, spreadsheets) and its source system — AS 1105.10, SAPA 11(f), 2024 Spotlight.
  12. Dependency on ITGCs and the in-scope application(s) — AS 2201.47/.B29, AS 2110.B1–.B5, SEC 33-8810 §II.A.1.d.
  13. Evidence of operation (documentary vs not) — AS 2201.51, AS 2315.32; SEC "reasonable support."
  14. ELC vs process-level and preventive vs detective — labels are permitted but **not decisive** for selection (AS 2201.41); still needed to evaluate design (SEC fn 30) and precision.
  15. Location/business unit — AS 2201.33 and Appendix B.
- "Key control" is not a term defined in AS 2201; the closest standard concept is the control "important to the auditor's conclusion" (AS 2201.39) / controls "management concludes are adequate to address the financial reporting risks" (SEC 33-8810). A key/non-key flag is therefore practice shorthand for "selected for testing/reliance."
- ITGC domain picklists should use the SEC four-area language (program development, program changes, computer operations, access to programs and data); AS 2201.B29 uses three of them (program changes, access to programs, computer operations).

## Q2. What fields do Big 4 / major firms, the IIA and ISACA recommend for RCMs and ITGC matrices?

### Takeaway
I found no IIA, ISACA, CAQ or Big 4 publication that prescribes a canonical RCM column list publicly; firm templates are proprietary. What is public converges on: risk ID/description, assertion, account, process, control ID/description (who/what/when/how/evidence), owner, frequency, type (preventive/detective), nature (manual/automated/IT-dependent), key flag, IPE/key reports, ITGC dependencies, and for ITGC matrices the in-scope application plus its supporting layers (database, OS, network). The IIA's GAIT methodology supplies the authoritative basis for the "layers" concept; ISACA Journal and KPMG (2026) supply ITGC scoping/risk attributes.

### Cited Findings
- **IIA GAIT (Guide to the Assessment of IT Risk):** ITGC process risks "exist in processes and at various IT layers" — application code, database, operating systems, and network; risks are mitigated by achieving IT control objectives, not individual controls; scoping starts by identifying the critical functionality of financially significant applications, and a layer is in scope only if failure there could reasonably affect that critical functionality. GAIT principles were drafted to align with AS 5. [PRACTICE — IIA] — [IIA GAIT Methodology page](https://iiadevecommerce-staging.azurewebsites.net/gait-methodology); [Accounting Today: IIA Releases IT Audit Guidance](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/iia-releases-it-audit-guidance)
- GAIT status: a Norman Marks post reports the IIA "decided to 'retire' the Methodology" due to few downloads, while saying it remains valid; current IIA status unconfirmed. [PRACTICE] — [Norman Marks on GAIT (mirror)](https://risk-academy.ru/?p=52321)
- **ISACA Journal (2010), "An Approach Toward Sarbanes-Oxley ITGC Risk Assessment":** recommends risk-assessing each in-scope application per ITGC process area ("change management, logical access, computer operations, job scheduling, and third parties/service organizations"). Integrity-risk parameters: number of changes; degree of automation/reliance on automated controls; commercial vs homegrown (internal developer access to source); number of developers. Access-risk parameters: number of users; "Direct access to the underlying database" ("can leave backdoor entries"); authentication mechanism (integrated OS authentication = higher risk). Uses a Low/Medium/High scale per application. [PRACTICE — ISACA] — [ISACA Journal 2010](https://www.isaca.org/resources/isaca-journal/past-issues/2010/an-approach-toward-sarbanes-oxley-itgc-risk-assessment)
- **KPMG, "Mastering IT Scoping for SOX Compliance" (2026):** system scoping criteria ("five-question litmus test") — whether the system "handles financial data, executes key controls, produces IPE, enables privileged bypass, or is an outsourced SOX-relevant system"; a system is in scope if it "Processes financial transactions (initiates, records, processes, or reports them), or Is relied upon by key manual controls (for approvals, reviews, reconciliations, etc.)"; "A complete SOX IT scope includes not only core financial applications but also the critical enabling tools that are relied upon to secure them." Webcast poll (2,900+ audit/risk leaders): user access review deficiencies (36%) and key report/IPE deficiencies (28%) are the top SOX IT challenges; 65% report more systems in SOX scope. [PRACTICE — Big 4] — [KPMG 2026 PDF](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2026/mastering-it-scoping-for-sox-compliance.pdf)
- **CAQ / FERF on management review controls:** best practice is for management to document "a risk assessment relating to each complex MRC" identifying which "attributes, or elements, of the MRC ... are most critical"; "it is important for management to understand the lineage of the data used in MRCs"; documentation "should focus the evidence of the data's reliability (e.g., its completeness and accuracy) for those key elements." Suggested actions include "Establish timelines for control performance" and "Consider the nature and extent of documentation of the MRC review." [PRACTICE — CAQ] — [CAQ MRC Roundtable Summary (Apr. 2021)](https://www.thecaq.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/caq_management-review-controls-roundtable-summary_2021-04.pdf); underlying study [Perspectives on MRCs (CAQ/FERF, Nov. 2020)](https://erm.ncsu.edu/az/erm/i/chan/library/caq-perspectives-on-management-controls-2020-11.pdf)
- **Practitioner/template sources (lower authority):** RCM templates commonly include control owner, frequency, preventive/detective, manual/automated/IT-dependent, evidence produced; nature values "Manual, Automated, or IT-Dependent Manual"; separate "IPE Used" and "ITGC Dependencies" columns; a "Key Reports and IPE" section capturing reports used, completeness/accuracy checks, and system dependencies; ITGC matrices add in-scope applications, databases, and operating systems per control. [PRACTICE — vendor/blog] — [Docsie RCM template](https://www.docsie.io/assets/templates/accounting-audit/risk-control-matrix/template.md); [SecurityScientist SOX ITGC matrix template](https://www.securityscientist.net/blog/sox-itgc-matrix-template/); [SmartSuite: internal controls RCM](https://www.smartsuite.com/guides/internal-controls-risk-control-matrix-control-testing)
- **Key control:** one practitioner source says a control is key "only in relation to a stated risk and assertion," with the test being whether the risk would go unaddressed if that control failed. [PRACTICE] — [SmartSuite: What is a key control](https://www.smartsuite.com/what-is/what-is-a-key-control)
- **ITGC domain contents (practice):** access to programs and data (provisioning/deprovisioning, authentication, SoD, privileged access, periodic access reviews); program changes (request, authorization, testing, approval, migration); program development (project approval, design, testing, data conversion, UAT); computer operations (job scheduling, batch processing, incident/problem management, backup/recovery). [PRACTICE — secondary] — [Wikipedia: Information technology controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology_controls); [SmartSuite: ITGCs](https://www.smartsuite.com/what-is/what-are-it-general-controls-itgc)

## Q3. What are common data-quality problems in client RCMs that auditors flag?

### Takeaway
The documented problems cluster into: (1) vague control descriptions lacking who/what/when/how/threshold/evidence, (2) controls not tied to the specific risk/assertion they address (or risks with no control), (3) reliance on review-type/ELC controls without demonstrated precision, (4) missing identification of IPE/system reports and their source systems, (5) IT-dependent controls not linked to the ITGCs and applications they rely on (including custom queries outside ITGC scope), and (6) missing evidence-of-performance definition. Items 3–5 are directly evidenced by PCAOB inspection observations; item 1 by practitioner sources.

### Cited Findings
- PCAOB inspections: firms "placed undue emphasis on testing management review controls and other detective controls without considering whether they adequately addressed the assessed risks of material misstatement," and "failed to test controls for all the" relevant assertions. [STAFF] — [PCAOB SAPA 11](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)
- PCAOB inspections: failure to "(1) test ITGCs that are important to the effective operation of the applications that generated the data or reports, (2) test the logic of the queries (or parameters) used to extract data ..., or (3) address control deficiencies ... with respect to the ITGCs"; and "IT-dependent controls used customized data or queries that were not subject to the ITGCs the firms tested." (RCM implication: report/query and its system must be recorded and linked.) [STAFF] — [PCAOB SAPA 11](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)
- ~17% of 2021 and 2022 inspection comment forms had IPC accuracy/completeness deficiencies; specific observation: auditor "did not identify and test controls over the accuracy and completeness of data used by the control owner in the operation of a control." [STAFF] — [PCAOB 2024 Data and Reports Spotlight](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/documents/data-and-reports-spotlight.pdf)
- Practitioners still rank key report/IPE deficiencies as the #2 SOX IT challenge (28%) after user access review deficiencies (36%), as of a Feb. 2026 KPMG poll; KPMG's Sue King: "The whole concept of IPE first came out in 2012... we're still struggling with it." [PRACTICE] — [KPMG 2026](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2026/mastering-it-scoping-for-sox-compliance.pdf)
- Roll-forward problems: interim testing of "highly subjective controls" updated to year-end with "general inquires" only. (RCM implication: risk/complexity attributes should be explicit so roll-forward effort can be set.) [STAFF] — [PCAOB SAPA 11](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)
- Precision gaps: an entity-level control may not suffice "if the amount of potential misstatement that could exist before being detected by the monitoring control is too high"; a control that "investigates items that are near the threshold for financial statement materiality has less precision." (RCM implication: missing threshold/criteria for investigation is a design-evaluation gap.) [STD-SEC / STAFF] — [SEC 33-8810](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf); [SAPA 11](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)
- MRC expectation gaps: absent a documented risk assessment of complex MRC inputs, "the auditor may believe that more assumptions and/or inputs are critical than does management, potentially resulting in an expectation gap." [PRACTICE — CAQ] — [CAQ MRC Roundtable Summary](https://www.thecaq.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/caq_management-review-controls-roundtable-summary_2021-04.pdf)
- Vague descriptions: a BDO piece on income-tax controls (per search summary; not fetched in full) flags that a statement like "management reviews the calculations" gives too little detail to show what review was performed, and that controls often lack specificity about evidence. [PRACTICE — mid-tier firm, partially verified] — [BDO: Four Common Flaws Related to Income Tax Controls](https://www.bdo.com/getmedia/81044931-8339-4998-b24b-1eb7d422ec35/TAX_Four-Common-Flaws-Related-Income-Tax-Controls.pdf)
- Practitioner sources: a testable description states what is done, who does it, how often, what information is used, the investigation threshold, and what evidence exists; "management reviews financial results monthly" is "too vague to test meaningfully." [PRACTICE — low authority] — [Umbrex: Internal Controls and SOX Compliance](https://umbrex.com/resources/governance-risk-and-compliance-system-playbook/internal-controls-and-sox-compliance/)
- ISACA: one-size-fits-all ITGC testing ignores per-application risk differences (e.g., an app may be high change-risk but low access-risk); missing application attributes (homegrown vs commercial, direct DB access, authentication method, user/developer counts) prevent risk-based ITGC scoping. [PRACTICE — ISACA] — [ISACA Journal 2010](https://www.isaca.org/resources/isaca-journal/past-issues/2010/an-approach-toward-sarbanes-oxley-itgc-risk-assessment)

### Inferences
- **Candidate automated data-quality checks for an RCM [INFERENCE]:**
  - Control description missing performer role, action verb, timing/frequency, object (what is reviewed/reconciled), threshold/criteria (for review controls), or evidence artifact.
  - Description frequency contradicts frequency field (e.g., text says "monthly", field says "quarterly").
  - Control with no linked risk; risk with no linked control (potential design gap per AS 2201.A3); significant account/relevant assertion with no key control.
  - Control mapped to an assertion not listed as relevant for any linked account.
  - Nature = automated or IT-dependent but no application/system and no linked ITGC.
  - IPE referenced in the description ("report", "listing", "aging", "query", "spreadsheet") but no IPE record/source system.
  - Review/ELC/MRC control with no precision attributes (threshold, aggregation level, expectation basis).
  - Fraud or significant risk with only detective, ELC, or low-precision controls.
  - Performer = reviewer for the same control (SoD signal); owner field blank or a person's name without role.
  - Application in scope but missing layer records (DB/OS) or ITGC domain coverage (e.g., no access or change controls).
  - Evidence field blank or "N/A" for manual controls (AS 2315.32: sampling not applicable when no documentary evidence).

### Gaps
- I found no PCAOB or Big 4 public statistic on the frequency of **specific** RCM defects (e.g., "% of client RCMs with vague descriptions"). Evidence is qualitative or limited to inspection categories (IPE ~17% of comment forms).
- Full BDO and Big 4 publications on control-description quality were not retrieved; the BDO point above comes from a search summary.

  - *IPE type* [PRACTICE/INFERENCE from SAPA 11]: Standard (canned) report; Custom report; Query/ad hoc extract; Spreadsheet/EUC; Data provided by service organization.
