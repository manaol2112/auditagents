# Optro hides its RCM schema, so standards must define it

Optro is not a new vendor. It is **AuditBoard under a new name, announced March 9, 2026**. The company began in 2014 as SOXHUB, and its SOX risk and control matrix (RCM) lives in the **Controls Management** module, which is the former SOXHUB. Optro publishes almost nothing about how that RCM is structured. The help center, developer portal, community and out-of-the-box RCM template all sit behind a customer login. The only control field names visible in public are **`id`, `name`, `uid` and `subprocess_id`**, and they come from an unofficial GitHub toolkit, not from the vendor. A canonical RCM schema therefore cannot be reverse-engineered from Optro. It has to rest on audit standards and then be checked against a real client export. No standard prescribes an RCM or its columns. Read together, though, PCAOB AS 2201, AS 2110 and AS 1105, SEC Release 33-8810 and Staff Audit Practice Alert No. 11 require a **traceable chain: significant account → relevant assertion → "what could go wrong" risk → control → control attributes**. Those attributes are nature, frequency, performer, precision, IT dependency, information used and evidence of operation. About two dozen RCM fields carry that chain. Among comparable platforms, only Diligent One (formerly HighBond) publishes exact import columns and mandatory-field rules. ServiceNow publishes form labels and picklists, while Workiva, Archer and Optro keep theirs behind logins. **No platform publicly documents a dedicated field for assertions, IPE (information produced by the entity), fraud risk or ITGC domain.** Those are the attributes PCAOB inspections most often find mishandled: IPE deficiencies appeared in roughly 17% of 2021 and 2022 inspection comment forms. The mapping agent's hardest job is pulling these attributes out of custom fields and free text, and the automated checks should concentrate there.

## Optro is AuditBoard renamed, with its RCM inside the former SOXHUB

The vendor's press release says it directly: "Today, AuditBoard becomes Optro." The release calls the change "same company, same products, same customers," and Accounting Today reported the rebrand independently at the IIA's Great Audit Minds conference ([PR Newswire](https://tools.prnewswire.com/en-us/live/20823/release/20260309EN03678); [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/auditboard-now-styles-itself-optro)). The vendor's own timeline runs from **SOXHUB (2014)** to **AuditBoard (2017)**, then **acquisition by Hg (2024)**, **FairNow (2025)**, **Midship (2026)** and **Optro (March 2026)** ([Optro AI info page](https://optro.ai/ai-info-for-llms)). Hg's 2024 deal valued the company at more than $3B. At that point it cited 2,000+ enterprise customers and "nearly half of the Fortune 500" ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/hg-agrees-to-buy-risk-firm-auditboard-in-3-billion-deal); [Hg](https://hgcapital.com/insights/auditboard-agrees-to-be-acquired-by-hg/)). It now claims more than 50% of the Fortune 500 ([PR Newswire](https://tools.prnewswire.com/en-us/live/20823/release/20260309EN03678)). Its preferred implementation partners include Deloitte, EY, Protiviti and RSM ([Optro AI info page](https://optro.ai/ai-info-for-llms)), so external auditors will see Optro-format RCMs often. Analyst standing (Leader in Gartner's 2025 Magic Quadrant for GRC Tools, Assurance Leaders, and in Forrester's 2026 GRC Wave) is vendor-reported only. The name is not ambiguous in this context. "OPTRO 2026" also appears as an optics event listed by FISBA, but no other GRC vendor uses the name ([FISBA](https://www.fisba.com/en/events/optro-2026)).

The legacy names still show up in the product, and a mapping agent should treat them as aliases. Optro's SOX page still captions dashboard images "SOXHUB." Login still goes to `login.auditboardapp.com` and support to `support.soxhub.com` ([Optro SOX page](https://optro.ai/product/sox-management)). The vendor lists **11 products**. The SOX/ICFR RCM belongs to Controls Management, which covers SOX, FDICIA, MAR, J-SOX and the UK Corporate Governance Code. Autonomous Testing (from Midship) plugs into it. CrossComply holds framework controls such as SOC 2, ISO 27001 and NIST, and RiskOversight holds enterprise risk ([Optro AI info page](https://optro.ai/ai-info-for-llms)). A September 2026 third-party review confirms that "Controls Management" is "the former SOXHUB." It also notes that all modules share "one data model," so one control library can serve SOX and operational audit at once ([Internal Audit Guide](https://internalauditguide.com/2026/09/optro-review/)). This has a practical consequence. A client's ITGCs could sit in Controls Management (if they are tested for SOX) or in CrossComply (if they run through a framework), and only the client's export shows which.

The marketing describes the RCM's purpose but not its structure. It promises an "out-of-the-box (OOTB) SOX RCM," "AI-generated narratives and flowcharts [that] map in risks and controls," and AI agents that "independently run attribute tests across uploaded evidence" ([Optro SOX page](https://optro.ai/product/sox-management)). Midship completed its acquisition around May 6–7, 2026 and is being folded into Controls Management, with a claimed "up to 87%" reduction in manual SOX effort ([Infor Capital](https://inforcapital.com/news/optro-acquires-ai-native-sox-automation-platform-midship-to-advance-agentic-audit-transformation/)). None of the public pages mention ICFR, walkthroughs, scoping, financial statement assertions or account-to-control mapping. That absence matters: these are the elements the standards treat as central.

## Four public field names are all Optro's RCM schema reveals

The most concrete evidence of Optro's data model comes from `markspivey/AuditBoard-Controls-Bulk-Delete`, an unofficial Python toolkit for administrators of the Control module. It calls the real REST API at `https://{tenant}.auditboardapp.com/api/v1` with bearer-token authentication ([GitHub README](https://github.com/markspivey/AuditBoard-Controls-Bulk-Delete); [api_client.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/markspivey/AuditBoard-Controls-Bulk-Delete/main/scripts/core/api_client.py)). Microsoft's SSO tutorial uses the same `/api/v1` path on `auditboardapp.com`, which corroborates it ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/saas-apps/auditboard-tutorial)). The toolkit deletes objects in dependency order: **Controls → Subprocesses → Processes → Entities → Region**. It also reads three join resources, `processes_data`, `subprocesses_data` and `controls_data`, which place library objects under specific entities ([analyze_region.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/markspivey/AuditBoard-Controls-Bulk-Delete/main/scripts/discovery/analyze_region.py); [find_dependencies.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/markspivey/AuditBoard-Controls-Bulk-Delete/main/scripts/discovery/find_dependencies.py)). One source that circulates should be discarded. Supergood's "AuditBoard API" page lists control fields such as "key/ITGC flags," but the page itself states that "Nothing on this page describes an existing AuditBoard product" ([Supergood](https://supergood.ai/docs/auditboard-api)).

The structure points to a **library-and-instance pattern**. Processes, subprocesses and controls are defined once and then deployed to each entity through the `_data` joins. Two things follow if that reading is right. Per-entity attributes such as owner or scoping status probably live on `controls_data`. And each library control belongs to exactly one subprocess, because `subprocess_id` is a single foreign key. The table below separates what is documented from what is inferred.

### Bulk import runs through an Excel add-in, and export through CSV

Optro has two bulk-data paths. The **"AuditBoard Import Tools" Excel add-in** on Microsoft AppSource "allows authorized AuditBoard users to leverage Microsoft Excel for generating data to be imported," and reportedly Customer Support has to enable it ([AppSource](https://appsource.microsoft.com/en-us/product/web-apps/WA200006771)). **Visual Import** is "a bulk change feature available to all customers" for updating existing records ([AuditBoard community events](https://community.auditboard.com/t5/events/bg-p/Events2/page/5)). For export, customers "can export all data in standard formats such as ZIP, PDF and CSV." The same review found no documented import path for historical workpapers ([Internal Audit Guide](https://internalauditguide.com/2026/09/optro-review/)). A low-confidence review summary says SOXHUB CSV exports carry control UIDs, test timing and titles, with significance, test types and status in separate files ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/148230/SOXHUB/reviews)). If that is true, a mapping agent has to join several files on UID before it can see a whole RCM row. The official developer portal (developer.auditboard.com) redirects to a login ([BlinkOps](https://docs.blinkops.com/docs/integrations/auditboard)). An **MCP server launched April 28, 2026** lets approved AI tools query live Optro data, "control matrices" included, under each user's permissions ([Internal Audit Guide](https://internalauditguide.com/2026/09/optro-review/); [SecurityBrief](https://securitybrief.news/story/optro-launches-mcp-server-for-governed-grc-data-access); [Optro AI info page](https://optro.ai/ai-info-for-llms)). For a client with access, this may be the most direct way to get the real schema.

Pre-built content exists but is gated. The OOTB SOX RCM is "pre-loaded" and requires "you must be a current Optro Customer," and EY sector-based RCMs are offered through the Exchange ([Optro Exchange](https://optro.ai/exchange/template-ootb-sox-rcm); [EY sector RCMs](https://auditboard.com/exchange/ey-sector-based-risk-and-control-matrices-rcms)). By inference from the data model, an import row probably needs the parent process and subprocess UIDs plus an entity assignment, with UIDs acting as matching keys. The add-in approach also suggests the template is generated from each tenant's configuration rather than fixed. Diligent's importer works that way, but this is analogy, not evidence.

## Six platforms name the same RCM concepts in six ways

Vendors publish very different amounts of detail. Only **Diligent One (HighBond)** publishes exact bulk-import column names and mandatory-field rules. Its controls template uses `referenceId`, `objectiveId`, `title`, `description`, `controlId`, `owner`, `ownerUser`, `frequency`, `method`, `controlType`, `preventDetect`, `status`, `position`, `entities` and `customAttributes_n_term/value`. Test columns repeat up to four times, for example `controlTests_1_sampleSize` and `controlTests_n_testingConclusion` ([Diligent One controls import](https://help.diligentoneplatform.com/helpdocs/d1p/en-us/Content/administration/bulk_importer/bulk_uploading_controls.htm)). Risks add `riskId`, `impact`, `likelihood` and `customFactors_n` ([Diligent One risks import](https://help.diligentoneplatform.com/helpdocs/d1p/en-us/Content/administration/bulk_importer/bulk_uploading_risks.htm)).

**ServiceNow** stores controls in `sn_compliance_control` and documents the form labels: Number, Name, Entity, Control Objective, Owner, Owning group, Key control, Weighting, Classification, Type, Category, Frequency and Enforcement ([ServiceNow: Create a control](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/governance-risk-compliance/policy-and-compliance-management/t_CreateAControl.html)). **Workiva's** ICFR solution uses single-select columns named Location, Owner, Process, Sub-process, Significance, Frequency, Auto/Manual and Preventive/Detective ([Workiva Support](https://support.workiva.com/hc/en-us/articles/35995752376724-Edit-risks-and-controls)). **Archer** documents only record tabs (Control Details, Risk Management, Evidence Information, Mappings, Testing) and sends customers to a Data Dictionary held by the account rep ([Archer IT Controls Assurance](https://help.archerirm.cloud/itsecrisk_itcontrols_2024.03/en-us/Content/Solutions/ITSecRisk/itsrm_itctrl_design.htm)). **SAP Process Control** arranges master data as a process, subprocess and control hierarchy. It links accounts to assertions through account groups, and "only the risk templates are used to define the organization's overall risk and control matrix" ([SAP Learning](https://learning.sap.com/courses/discovering-the-key-functionalities-of-sap-process-control/explaining-master-data)). MetricStream was not researched.

### The header "Control Type" carries three different meanings

The most dangerous header is "Control Type." In client RCMs it usually means preventive/detective. In HighBond, `controlType` sits next to a separate `preventDetect` column and so probably holds manual/automated values. In Archer, "type" means Technical versus Process, which decides which testing options appear ([Archer](https://help.archerirm.cloud/regcorp_ctrlprg_2024.03/en-us/Content/Solutions/RegCorpComp/rccm_ctrlprg_ctrlprocedure_creating.htm)). ServiceNow's Type and Category are IT-compliance taxonomies with values such as "Technical security" and "Systems continuity" ([ServiceNow control objectives](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/Z61H6JNUJXKe8NNPQzK7MQ/aP~2QUBpT6pgCWxV18pViQ)), so they should never receive a SOX "Manual" value. The mapper should classify ambiguous columns by the values they contain, not by the header text.

### Mandatory rules and link syntax are documented only for Diligent and ServiceNow

Diligent rejects any row with a blank mandatory field. For controls the mandatory fields are `referenceId` (unique, ≤255 characters), `objectiveId`, `description` and `controlId`. `frequency`, `controlType` and `preventDetect` become mandatory depending on the project type, and dropdown values must "match the options exactly" ([Diligent One controls import](https://help.diligentoneplatform.com/helpdocs/d1p/en-us/Content/administration/bulk_importer/bulk_uploading_controls.htm)). Risks require `referenceId`, `objectiveId` and `description` ([Diligent One risks import](https://help.diligentoneplatform.com/helpdocs/d1p/en-us/Content/administration/bulk_importer/bulk_uploading_risks.htm)). Diligent's `highbond.com` API endpoints remain supported only until **December 16, 2026** ([Diligent API docs](https://docs-apis.highbond.com/)). ServiceNow makes Entity "a mandatory field on the Control form" ([ServiceNow: Manage controls](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/zurich/governance-risk-compliance/grc-compliance-management-workspace/manage-controls-ws.html)). Its Frequency field is documented with the values Event Driven, Daily, Weekly, Monthly, Quarterly, Semi-Annually and Annually, and is "for reporting purposes only" ([ServiceNow: Create a control](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/governance-risk-compliance/policy-and-compliance-management/t_CreateAControl.html)).

Many-to-many links arrive in three forms. The first is a delimited ID list in a single cell, like Diligent's comma-separated `entities`. The second is a separate mapping table, which is how ServiceNow links controls to entities and requirements ([ServiceNow Community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/grc-forum/what-the-the-following-tables-used-for/m-p/3030481)). The third, typical of flat client spreadsheets, repeats a control row for each risk it addresses. Diligent's documented importer has no direct risk-to-control column; risks and controls meet only through their shared `objectiveId`.

Header style is a reliable fingerprint of the source system. camelCase headers with `_n_` repeat groups point to Diligent. Diligent custom attributes arrive as term/value column pairs, so a semantic name like "Assertions" is the value of a `customAttributes_n_term` cell, and the mapper has to pivot those pairs. Title-case "Number / Control Objective / Entity / Key control / Classification" points to a ServiceNow list export. "Auto/Manual / Significance / Sub-process" points to Workiva. By inference, "UID" with a Region, Entity, Process and Subprocess hierarchy points to Optro. Workiva SOX data moves by copy/paste, Wdesk Sync or the Wdata API (`POST /api/v1/table/{tableId}/spreadsheet/import`, XLSX ≤16MB) ([Workiva developers](https://developers.workiva.com/wdata-v1/wdata-importfromspreadsheets.html); [Workiva support](https://support.workiva.com/hc/en-us/articles/360035643172-Create-or-import-a-spreadsheet)). SAP Process Control uses the Master Data Upload Generator or Content Lifecycle Management ([SAP Learning](https://learning.sap.com/courses/discovering-the-key-functionalities-of-sap-process-control/creation-and-management-of-master-data)).

## Standards demand a traceable chain of about 25 data points

**No PCAOB or SEC text prescribes an RCM or a column list.** The RCM is a practice artifact. Each critical column exists because it carries a data point the standards require the auditor to understand, or that management must support. The obligations fall on different parties. PCAOB "should" language binds the auditor. Management's obligation comes from SEC Release 33-8810: "management must maintain reasonable support for its assessment," and "documentation of the design of the controls … is an integral part of the reasonable support." The form can be flexible: "policy manuals, process models, flowcharts, job descriptions" ([SEC 33-8810 §II.A.1.e](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)). "Mandatory" therefore means the data must exist somewhere in management's documentation. The RCM is where auditors expect to find it.

The chain starts top-down. The auditor "should identify significant accounts and disclosures and their relevant assertions" (AS 2201.28). It then determines "the likely sources of potential misstatements" by asking "what could go wrong?" (AS 2201.30). The auditor understands how transactions "are initiated, authorized, processed, and recorded," including points where fraud could arise (AS 2201.34). Controls are selected because they address "the assessed risk of misstatement to each relevant assertion" (AS 2201.39). The mapping is explicitly many-to-many: "one control might address the assessed risk of misstatement to more than one relevant assertion" (AS 2201.40). Selection turns on the risk addressed, "rather than on how the control is labeled" (AS 2201.41) ([PCAOB Release 2007-005A](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)).

**AS 2201.47 is the strongest basis for control attribute columns.** It names the factors that set the risk associated with a control. They include "the nature of the control and the frequency with which it operates," "the degree to which the control relies on the effectiveness of other controls (e.g., … information technology general controls)," "the competence of the personnel who perform the control," "whether the control relies on performance by an individual or is automated," and "the complexity of the control and the significance of the judgments." AS 2110.59 adds the risk-side fields: affected accounts and assertions, likelihood and magnitude, and whether a risk is significant. It specifies that significance rests "on inherent risk, without regard to the effect of controls." AS 2110.71 adds that "a fraud risk is a significant risk" ([PCAOB AS 2110](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2110)). Two pending amendments, to AS 2201 ¶.09 (with a new ¶.99) and to AS 2110 ¶¶.05 and .41, take effect December 15, 2026 and do not touch these paragraphs ([PCAOB AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201)).

The canonical field set below is a synthesis of AS 2201 ([Release 2007-005A](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)), AS 2110 ([PCAOB](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2110)), AS 1105 ([PCAOB](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS1105)), AS 2315 ([PCAOB](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2315)), SEC 33-8810 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)), SAPA 11 ([PCAOB](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)) and the 2024 Data and Reports Spotlight ([PCAOB](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/documents/data-and-reports-spotlight.pdf)). "Required by implication" means a standard requires the data point, though not as a column. "Recommended" means the standard defines or invites the field. "Practice" means it is needed for traceability or workflow but has no standards basis. Suggested canonical names are proposals.

| Canonical field | Object | Applies to | Status | Basis (paragraph) |

"Key control" is not defined anywhere in AS 2201. The closest concept is a control "important to the auditor's conclusion" (AS 2201.39). A key/non-key flag is shorthand for "relied upon." Practitioners define it as key "only in relation to a stated risk and assertion" ([SmartSuite](https://www.smartsuite.com/what-is/what-is-a-key-control)). It should therefore sit on the risk-control link rather than on the control alone. That placement is my inference, but it follows from AS 2201.40's many-to-many framing.

The entities and links follow from the standards. At minimum the model needs Entity, Process/Subprocess, Account, Risk, Control, Application, ITGC and IPE records, joined by Risk↔Control, Risk↔Assertion/Account, Control↔Application, Control↔ITGC and Control↔IPE link tables. Optro appears to allow only one parent subprocess per library control. A canonical model allows a control under several processes, so exporting it back to Optro could require duplicating controls. A blank template built from this schema works best as linked sheets (Risks, Controls, Risk-Control Map, Applications/ITGCs, IPE). A single flat sheet forces the repeated-row pattern that breaks traceability.

### ITGCs exist only to support other controls

The SEC limits management to "those IT general controls that are necessary for the proper and consistent operation of other controls designed to adequately address financial reporting risks." It names four areas: "program development, program changes, computer operations, and access to programs and data." It also says ITGCs that "primarily pertain to efficiency or effectiveness" need not be evaluated ([SEC 33-8810 §II.A.1.d](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)). AS 2110.B4 supplies a ready-made ITGC risk library from the standard itself. It covers inaccurate processing; unauthorized access to data; IT personnel access beyond their duties; unauthorized changes to master files; unauthorized changes to systems or programs; failure to make necessary changes; inappropriate manual intervention; and loss of data ([PCAOB AS 2110](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2110)).

The IIA's GAIT methodology adds the layer concept. Risks "exist in processes and at various IT layers," meaning application, database, operating system and network. A layer is in scope only if a failure there could affect critical financial functionality ([IIA GAIT](https://iiadevecommerce-staging.azurewebsites.net/gait-methodology)). The IIA's current endorsement of GAIT is uncertain. ISACA recommends risk-rating each application per ITGC area using attributes such as number of changes, commercial versus homegrown, direct database access, authentication method and user counts ([ISACA Journal 2010](https://www.isaca.org/resources/isaca-journal/past-issues/2010/an-approach-toward-sarbanes-oxley-itgc-risk-assessment)). KPMG's 2026 scoping test asks whether a system "handles financial data, executes key controls, produces IPE, enables privileged bypass, or is an outsourced SOX-relevant system" ([KPMG 2026](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2026/mastering-it-scoping-for-sox-compliance.pdf)). An ITGC record therefore needs at least these fields: application → layer → domain → ITGC risk → control (owner, frequency, nature, evidence) → the dependent application controls, IT-dependent controls and IPE it supports. The final link is the one that matters most.

### Business process controls rest on assertion mapping and evidence

For transaction-level controls, the decisive fields are the assertion link, the risk description, nature, frequency and evidence. AS 2201.51 makes testability depend on "whether the operation of the control results in documentary evidence of its operation." AS 2315.32 says sampling "generally is not applicable to tests of controls that … provide no documentary evidence of performance" ([PCAOB AS 2315](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2315)). Frequency defines the population, but the standard sets the drivers of sample size (tolerable rate, expected deviation rate, allowable risk), not frequency-based numbers. Practitioner sample-size tables conflict, so they should not be hard-coded as "standard" ([SOC2Auditors](https://soc2auditors.org/insights/soc-2-sample-size/); [Wolters Kluwer](https://wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/audit-analytics-is-sampling-enough-for-internal-controls-testing)).

### Management review controls need six precision fields

SAPA 11 comes closest to an official attribute list for any control type. For a management review control, the auditor evaluates whether it addresses the risk to the relevant assertion, along with six precision factors: "objective of the review," "level of aggregation," "consistency of performance," "correlation to relevant assertions," "predictability of expectations," and "criteria for investigation." The auditor also evaluates "the steps involved in identifying, investigating, and resolving significant differences," the performer's "competence and authority," frequency, and "the information used in the review" ([PCAOB SAPA 11](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)). The SEC adds that a monitoring control is inadequate "if the amount of potential misstatement that could exist before being detected … is too high" ([SEC 33-8810](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf)). The CAQ recommends that management document "a risk assessment relating to each complex MRC" and understand "the lineage of the data used" ([CAQ MRC Roundtable](https://www.thecaq.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/caq_management-review-controls-roundtable-summary_2021-04.pdf)). An MRC row without a threshold and an expectation basis cannot be evaluated for design.

### ITACs need baseline and configuration fields to support benchmarking

Automated application controls get lower risk only "if relevant information technology general controls are effective" (AS 2201.47). Benchmarking applies when ITGCs over "program changes, access to programs, and computer operations" are effective and the auditor "verifies that the automated application control has not changed since the auditor established a baseline" (AS 2201.B29). Automated controls "may be dependent upon the related files, tables, data, and parameters" (B30). The factors include whether the control "can be matched to a defined program within an application" and whether program compilation-date reports are available (B31) ([PCAOB Release 2007-005A](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/rulemaking/docket_021/2007-06-12_release_no_2007-005a.pdf)). An ITAC record therefore needs the application, the program or configuration reference, the baseline date, any dependent tables or master data, and the linked ITGCs. The SEC's examples of automated controls are "automated matching, error checking or edit checking." Its examples of IT-dependent controls are "consistent application of a formula or performance of a calculation." Its example of a hybrid is a manual investigation of "a computer generated exception report" (fn 34–36). These examples give the mapper a test for classifying nature.

### IPE remains the most-cited failure point

Under AS 1105.10, when the auditor uses company-produced information, it must "test the accuracy and completeness of the information, or test the controls over the accuracy and completeness," and evaluate whether it is "sufficiently precise and detailed" ([PCAOB AS 1105](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS1105)). That quote is the pre-amendment text. Amendments to ¶.10 and a new ¶.10A (Release 2024-007) apply to fiscal years beginning on or after December 15, 2025, which means calendar-2026 audits. The amended wording was not retrieved and should be verified before it is quoted as current. SAPA 11 says the effectiveness of a control that uses system-generated reports "depends in part on the controls over the accuracy and completeness" of those reports. It cites inspection failures in testing "the logic of the queries (or parameters) used to extract data" and in "customized data or queries that were not subject to the ITGCs the firms tested" ([PCAOB SAPA 11](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)). The PCAOB's 2024 Spotlight found such deficiencies in **about 17% of comment forms in both 2021 and 2022** ([PCAOB Spotlight](https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/documents/data-and-reports-spotlight.pdf)). In a February 2026 KPMG poll of 2,900+ audit and risk leaders, key report and IPE problems ranked second (28%), behind user access reviews (36%) ([KPMG 2026](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2026/mastering-it-scoping-for-sox-compliance.pdf)). IPE therefore needs its own object, with these fields: report name, source system, report type (standard, custom, query or spreadsheet), parameters or query logic, completeness-and-accuracy procedure, linked ITGCs and consuming controls.

When normalizing frequency, the mapper should fold "As Needed," "Ad hoc," "Transactional" and "Per occurrence" into Event-driven, and "Yearly" into Annual. "Bi-annual" should be confirmed before mapping, because clients use it to mean both semi-annual and every two years. Event-driven controls need a population definition rather than a calendar count. "IT-Dependent Manual" is practice vocabulary; the standards' own words are "IT-dependent controls" (SAPA 11) and "dependent upon IT functionality" (SEC).

## Inspection failures translate directly into automated RCM checks

The defects that PCAOB inspections document fall into six groups:

1. Vague control descriptions.
2. Controls not tied to a specific risk and assertion.
3. Review controls without demonstrated precision.
4. Unidentified IPE.
5. IT-dependent controls not linked to their ITGCs.
6. Evidence of performance left undefined.

Inspectors found that firms "placed undue emphasis on testing management review controls and other detective controls without considering whether they adequately addressed the assessed risks," and "failed to test controls for all the" relevant assertions ([SAPA 11](https://pcaobus.org/Standards/QandA/10-24-2013_SAPA_11.pdf)). Practitioners flag descriptions such as "management reviews financial results monthly" as "too vague to test meaningfully" ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/governance-risk-and-compliance-system-playbook/internal-controls-and-sox-compliance/)). Under AS 2201.A3, a design deficiency exists when "a control necessary to meet the control objective is missing," so an unlinked risk is a potential design gap on its face. The checks below follow from these sources. The logic is my inference.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the obvious approach to the build. Optro's schema is gated and configurable per tenant, so it cannot serve as the master. It is one output target among several. The canonical schema should come from the standards' chain of account → assertion → risk → control → attributes, and Optro, Diligent, ServiceNow and Workiva should each be handled as adapters. This also locates the mapping agent's real value. Vendors agree on the low-stakes fields: ID, name, description, frequency, owner and preventive/detective. Every vendor leaves assertions, IPE, ITGC dependencies, precision and fraud flags to custom attributes or free text, and those are the fields regulators examine most closely. An agent that only renames columns will appear to succeed while dropping exactly what an inspection would test. It also has to pull these attributes out of description text and custom-attribute pairs.

The fastest next step is a single real Optro export, ideally with a field-configuration screenshot, or a schema query through Optro's MCP server. Either would turn most of the inferred Optro rows above into documented ones in a day. Until then, the blank template should follow the linked-sheet canonical model with standards-sourced picklists. Optro-specific labels should be configuration, not hard-coded. Diligent's December 16, 2026 API endpoint change and the 2026 effective dates of the AS 1105 amendments both argue for keeping vendor and standards references versioned rather than fixed.
